\subsection{Content-Derived Addressing by Random-Projection Quantization}
\label{sec:hash}

Specified design (current runs use equal-width scalar buckets).
Each block maps to an address via stateless random-projection quantizer (SimHash family).
Let $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ be a block embedding.
Fix $k$ seeded Gaussian vectors $r_1,\dots,r_k$ and $V$ quantization levels.
Address:
\[
h(x) = (q(\langle x, r_1 \rangle), \dots, q(\langle x, r_k \rangle)) \in [V]^k,
\]
where $q(\cdot)$ quantizes each projection to $V$ IDs.
Address space: $V^k$.
No table, no training: seeds specify the map, so any worker reproduces the address from content alone.
Nearby embeddings agree; distant ones decorrelate.

Under uniform addressing, $N$ blocks collide with probability
\[
P_{\mathrm{coll}} \approx 1 - \exp\!\left(-\frac{N(N-1)}{2V^k}\right).
\]
Requiring $P_{\mathrm{coll}} \le \delta$ gives $V^k \ge N(N-1)/(2\ln(1/(1-\delta)))$.
Concretely, for $N=10^5$, $V=32{,}000$, and $\delta=10^{-4}$, $k \ge 4$ suffices, since $V^4 \approx 10^{18} \gg N^2/(2\delta)$.

Embeddings are non-uniform: let $p_b$ be bin-$b$ mass and $\rho = \sum_b p_b^2$ the collision mass.
Then $P_{\mathrm{coll}} \le \binom{N}{2}\rho$, with effective capacity $V_{\mathrm{eff}}^k = 1/\rho$.
Skew shrinks capacity below $V^k$: equal-width bucketing concentrates mass (large $\rho$); projection quantization spreads dense regions across coordinates, keeping $\rho$ small and $V_{\mathrm{eff}}^k$ large.
