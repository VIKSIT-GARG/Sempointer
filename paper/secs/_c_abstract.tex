\begin{abstract}
Long-context inference feeds every token to attention, so cost grows with length. SemPointer proposes semantic indirection: blocks sit in an off-attention store while short pointers stay in the prompt; a dense encoder retrieves blocks by address and resolves them to text. This concept paper gives an indirection model, a pointer design with routing and fallback, a cost-quality theory, and testable predictions for precision, fallback, and breakeven length. Honest thesis: the instantiation ties dense retrieval-augmented generation at higher token cost, and pointers are empirically inert. Scope: concept only; no fidelity or superiority claims.
\end{abstract}
\keywords{long-context inference, dense retrieval, semantic addressing, negative results, benchmarking}
