\begin{abstract}
Full-context inference feeds every token to attention, so cost grows steeply with context length.
SemPointer instead stores context blocks in an off-attention text substrate and keeps short pointer strings in the active prompt; per query, an off-the-shelf dense encoder (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) selects blocks (k=8, m=1, B=512) by cosine similarity and resolves them to verbatim text.
The pointer strings themselves do not affect selection.
We measured Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (NF4, greedy decoding, seed 42) on RULER containment (n=180), LongBench F1 (n=1150), LoCoMo under a token-F1 adaptation (n=199), and a KV-compress pilot (n=10).
Against full context, SemPointer uses 10--15$\times$ fewer active tokens but lowers quality: RULER $-0.37$ (95\% CI $[-0.44,-0.30]$), LongBench $-0.033$ ($[-0.040,-0.027]$), LoCoMo $-0.012$ ($[-0.022,-0.003]$, not significant after Bonferroni correction).
Against dense RAG it ties on quality at higher token cost under identical m=1/representations, and the pointer strings contribute no measured quality effect. Scope: one model, one seed, text substrate only, contexts capped at 16k tokens.
\end{abstract}
